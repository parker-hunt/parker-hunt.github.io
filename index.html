About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Physics at Boston College and a graduate researcher at the Schiller Institute for Integrated Science and Society, advised by Dr. Yi Ming. My research applies fundamental mathematical and physical laws to understand the governing dynamics of time-mean divergent tropical circulations, specifically the Hadley and Walker circulations and their local counterparts. To better understand these circulations, I utilize an idealized moist general circulation model (GCM) to investigate the response to three distinct perturbations: zonally asymmetric (ZA) thermal forcings of varying magnitudes, ZA forcings of varying length scales, and elevated CO2 concentrations. Building on the insights gained from these idealized simulations, I have also collaborated with others to examine the tropical circulation in CMIP6 model ensembles and ERA5 reanalysis data.

I earned my B.S. in Physics with a minor in Mathematics in 2020 from The Ohio State University (Magna Cum Laude, with Research Distinction in Physics). My broader background spans several areas of theoretical and computational physics. At OSU, I conducted research on non-linear dynamical systems and stochastic PDEs with Dr. Ciriyam Jayaprakash, physics education research with Dr. Andrew Heckler and Dr. Gregory Kilcup, and experimental 2D materials with Dr. Jay Gupta. When I first arrived at Boston College I also worked on non-Hermitian second-quantized systems in theoretical condensed matter physics with Dr. Benedetta Flebus. Then finally settled into the world of atmospheric dynamics, joining Dr. Ming’s group. I am currently seeking postdoctoral opportunities starting in Summer/Fall 2027, where I aim to apply this rigorous physics background to investigate fundamental problems within the atmospheric system, with a particular interest in extreme events, wave-mean-flow interactions, and other non-linear phenomena.
