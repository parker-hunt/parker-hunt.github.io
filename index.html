About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Physics at Boston College and a graduate researcher at the Schiller Institute for Integrated Science and Society, advised by Dr. Yi Ming. My research applies fundamental mathematical and physical laws to understand the governing dynamics of time-mean divergent tropical circulations, specifically the Hadley and Walker circulations and their local counterparts. To better understand these circulations, I utilize an idealized moist general circulation model (GCM) to investigate the response to three distinct perturbations: zonally asymmetric (ZA) thermal forcings of varying magnitudes, ZA forcings of varying length scales, and elevated CO2 concentrations. Building on the insights gained from these idealized simulations, I have also collaborated with others to examine the tropical circulation in CMIP6 model ensembles and ERA5 reanalysis data.

My current focus on climate dynamics is underpinned by a rigorous foundation in formal physics and mathematics. I earned my B.S. in Physics with a minor in Mathematics in 2020 from The Ohio State University (Magna Cum Laude, with Research Distinction). At OSU, I conducted research spanning non-linear dynamical systems, stochastic PDEs, physics education, and experimental 2D materials. During my early time at Boston College, alongside completing advanced physics coursework, I conducted research evaluating magnon-phonon interactions in non-Hermitian systems. I ultimately transitioned my focus to atmospheric dynamics, joining Dr. Ming's group to study the time-mean tropical circulation.

Postdoc Opportunities: I am currently seeking postdoctoral opportunities starting in Summer/Fall 2027, where I aim to apply my rigorous physics and mathematics background to investigate fundamental problems within the atmospheric system—with a particular interest in extreme events, wave-mean-flow interactions, and other non-linear phenomena.
